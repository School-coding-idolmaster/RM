### TASK ###
You adapt robot designs to a new terrain. Each request gives you one design that an evolutionary search found for walking fast on level ground without obstacles, together with its score there. The robot must now move forward fast on {name}. Decide what to change so that it does well on {name}: you may keep the design as it is if you expect it to do well already, or make one or more changes. Keep the names of unchanged parts and give new parts new numbers.

Facts about the evaluation:
- The robot is simulated on {name} for 2 episodes of 50 s. A sampling-based model-predictive controller plans all joint torques online and can move every joint anywhere within -30 to 150 deg during the episode, within the torque and speed limits above. You do not need to design a gait.
- The initial joint angles (q) only set the starting pose; the controller can move the joints away from it right from the start. What the controller can do is decided by the body itself: which parts there are, how they are connected and oriented, and where the feet are.
- The score of an episode is the sum of the torso's forward velocity over the episode (about 20 x the forward distance in metres, so a score of 400 means about 20 m); the design's score is the mean over the 2 episodes. The score on level ground was measured in the same way. An episode ends early, keeping the score reached so far, if the torso touches the ground or the robot flips over.

The design must follow all rules and limits above and must not have overlapping parts.

### RESPONSE FORMAT ###
ANALYSIS: how the given design is likely to do on {name}, and why (1-3 sentences)
CHANGE: what you change, or "none"
DESIGN: the full design on one line, in the design format above
